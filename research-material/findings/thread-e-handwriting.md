**Arabic/RTL gap, stated plainly.** The largest public dataset for handwritten Arabic is only ~1,600 pages, and the widely used KHATT benchmark holds just 1,000 pages from 1,000 writers (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02179v2, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259092955_KHATT_An_open_Arabic_offline_handwritten_text_database). Community models exist (TrOCR fine-tunes, CRNNs on KHATT: https://huggingface.co/David-Magdy/TR_OCR_LARGE, https://github.com/FixFips/ArabicOCR_KHATT), but none is a production-grade, layout-aware Arabic handwriting recognizer. No open model matches MyScript or ML Kit on real Arabic handwriting. That is a fact, not a hedge.

**Verdict:** on clean, segmented English handwriting lines, TrOCR-large (2.89% CER on IAM) is genuinely close to commercial ink-engine quality. Nothing open combines line detection + layout + interactive ink + Arabic + phone-class latency in one package. The "everywhere, no setup" promise is not deliverable today from open models alone.

## 4. Ink-to-shape / palm rejection prior art in open source

**Palm rejection (Xournal++ lineage).** Xournal++ (https://github.com/xournalpp/xournalpp) ships palm rejection built on device classification: the touchscreen is assigned a tool (often "hand" or nothing) and touch is ignored while the stylus is in proximity, an approach inherited from the original Xournal, whose proximity flag had known failure modes (https://sourceforge.net/p/xournal/bugs/162/). It remains brittle in practice: settings tweaks have crashed Ubuntu/Wayland setups (https://github.com/xournalpp/xournalpp/issues/5721), inconsistency reports date to 2016 on Surface devices (https://github.com/xournalpp/xournalpp/issues/170), and users still work around it by mis-assigning devices in preferences (https://www.reddit.com/r/SurfaceLinux/comments/1762msq/palm_rejection_xournal_wayland/). A community patch (merged in a fork PR) shows the real fix shape: ignore touch entirely while the stylus touches the screen, reset gesture state so a resting palm cannot scroll the page or be misread as a pinch, and disable GTK kinetic scrolling, which otherwise grabs touches before the app can reject them (https://github.com/idoraz1/xournalpp/pull/7). Outside the app, desktop users fall back to xinput-level device toggling scripts (https://github.com/Patan98/palm-rejection). Lesson for a notes app: palm rejection is a device-classification plus event-sequencing problem, not AI; the hard part is per-platform input quirks, and the open-source prior art is battle-tested but fragile.

**Ratta Supernote ecosystem.** Ratta ships on-device real-time handwriting recognition (required for handwriting keyword search and page conversion, per firmware 2.20 notes: https://ewritable.net/brands/ratta-supernote/firmware/2-20/), with on-device re-recognition of a page (https://ratta.helpjuice.com/handwriting-recognition). The community's open-source tooling deliberately stays downstream of the device: supernote-tool converts .note files to PNG or vector PDF without a device (https://github.com/jya-dev/supernote-tool); an Obsidian plugin reads recognition sidecars only when the user enabled recognition on the device itself (https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/supernote-annotations); and cross-ecosystem search tools like UltraBridge offload recognition to cloud VLMs (ChatGPT/Claude), i.e., the community accepts cloud APIs rather than shipping its own recognizer (https://github.com/jdkruzr/ultrabridge). Lesson: even a device vendor with full hardware control licenses or builds recognition as a closed component; the open ecosystem wraps it rather than replaces it.

**Ink-to-shape.** Commercial interactive ink does this as a core feature: MyScript recognizes 10+ shape and connector types with SVG/GraphML export (https://www.myscript.com/sdk/); ML Kit's digital ink API classifies sketches and gestures (https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/digital-ink-recognition). An equivalent open-source engine (shape dictionary + gesture classification on stroke data) was not located in this pass: UNVERIFIED, and flagged as a real gap in the open stack.

## 5. Gap analysis: what "no setup handwriting everywhere" honestly costs today

1. **A free SDK exists, but only in Google's walled garden.** ML Kit Digital Ink Recognition gives free, on-device, 300+-language (Arabic included) handwriting recognition, yet only on Android/iOS, with closed models downloaded from Google (https://developers.googleblog.com/digital-ink-recognition-in-ml-kit/). A local-first, privacy-first app cannot verify or pin those models, and desktop and web have no equivalent.
2. **The full interactive-ink experience is quote-priced and proprietary.** MyScript's math, diagram, music, and editing-gesture stack has no free tier beyond 25 dev licenses, no public price, and per-device licensing that an open-source project cannot redistribute (https://developer.myscript.com/pricing).
3. **Open handwriting recognition is line-level, not page-level.** TrOCR reaches 2.89% CER on IAM but only on cropped single text lines (https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26538/26310); line segmentation, layout analysis, and ink UX are unbundled problems the app must solve itself.
4. **Arabic/RTL is the widest gap.** Commercial interactive ink mostly handles Arabic only in offscreen/batch mode (https://x.com/myscript, https://developer-support.myscript.com/support/discussions/topics/16000033357); ML Kit does it on-device on mobile; the open world has KHATT-trained line-level research models against a data famine of ~1,000–1,600 pages (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02179v2). No open option is production-grade for Arabic handwriting.
5. **Photo-of-paper OCR is a different, weaker product.** Tesseract and PaddleOCR degrade sharply on handwriting (20–40% and ~72.8% respectively: https://www.handwritingocr.com/blog/tesseract-vs-ai-ocr-handwriting, https://imagetotable.ai/blog/best-open-source-ocr-tools-2026). "Point your phone at your notebook" is not solved on-device; the cloud-LLM results (1.71% CER on IAM) show what is possible but require sending user pages to a paid cloud API (https://www.emerald.com/jd/article/81/7/334/1275080/Benchmarking-large-language-models-for-handwritten), which contradicts local-first.
6. **Palm rejection and shape recognition are solvable in-house; recognition of cursive is not.** The open prior art covers input handling well and recognition poorly.

## 6. Recommendation

Ship the promise in layers, and word the marketing honestly. Concretely: (a) use platform engines where they are good and free, ML Kit Digital Ink on Android/iOS and Apple Vision/VisionKit on Apple platforms, each fully on-device, while keeping the abstraction layer swappable; (b) for desktop and web, integrate TrOCR (ONNX/Transformers.js for browser, small variant for CPU) behind the same interface, framed as line-level "convert selection to text" rather than ambient whole-page recognition; (c) treat Arabic/RTL as a differentiator, not a checkbox: a KHATT/Arabic-fine-tuned TrOCR line recognizer inside a proper RTL layout engine would exceed what MyScript's interactive mode offers, since even MyScript relegates Arabic to offscreen recognition; (d) never ship Tesseract as the handwriting path; it is acceptable only as the printed-text fallback; (e) do not promise photo-of-paper transcription on-device today; if offered at all, route it as opt-in cloud processing or defer it. Avoid MyScript: the licensing shape (per-device, quote-only, closed assets) is incompatible with a local-first open-source app.
